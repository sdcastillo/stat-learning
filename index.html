How to read these notes

This is an unofficial exercise index for An Introduction to Statistical Learning by James, Witten, Hastie, and Tibshirani. The files follow the first-edition chapter order in the repository, from the bias–variance setup in Chapter 2 through principal components and clustering in Chapter 10. The book’s site is statlearning.com.

Conceptual notes keep the algebra. Chapter 4, Exercise 1 starts from the logistic curve and rearranges it until the odds equal p(X) / (1 − p(X)) = e^(β₀ + β₁X). Chapter 3, Exercise 7 proves that in simple linear regression, R² equals the square of the correlation between x and y, from the definitions of TSS and RSS. Chapter 10, Exercise 1 shows that the summed squared Euclidean distance inside a cluster is twice the within-cluster sum of squares, the identity used when a method minimizes within-cluster variance.

Applied answers are R, and several of them were knitted so the figures stay next to the code. The Chapter 2 script reads College.csv, marks a college elite when Top10perc is above 50, and later uses the Boston suburb data for crime, tax, and room counts. The Chapter 3 knitted page regresses mpg on horsepower in Auto.csv, takes the large F-statistic as evidence of a relationship, and reads the residual plots as a sign of nonlinearity.

Every chapter includes the book lab: an introduction to R; linear regression; logistic regression, LDA, QDA, and KNN on Smarket; cross-validation and the bootstrap; best subset, ridge, the lasso, PCR, and PLS on Hitters; polynomials, splines, and GAMs on Wage; trees through boosting on Carseats; support vector machines, including the gene-expression data; and PCA plus hierarchical clustering on NCI60. ch2/notes/ adds a curse-of-dimensionality plot and a hypercube-boundary simulation. ch9/9.R is a short quiz simulation that fits logistic regression in a setup usually compared with a linear support vector machine.

The previous home page sent the Chapter 2 answers and the early Chapter 3 answers to raw files on the upstream repository. Those notes are in this fork, so the links below open them here. Chapter 3, Exercise 7 has its own proof page. Exercises 8–15 share one knitted HTML file. A step marked (*) is a step the write-up treats as unsettled, the same convention as the README. Sam Castillo (UMass Amherst mathematics) publishes this copy. The upstream collection is asadoughi/stat-learning.

Takeaways

  • Odds, R², and cluster variance Chapter 4 Exercise 1, Chapter 3 Exercise 7, and Chapter 10 Exercise 1 write those identities out.
  • College, Auto, and Boston Applied R uses College.csv, Auto.csv, and the Boston suburbs, with knitted plots where a chapter was rendered.
  • Book labs sit in each chapter Resampling, ridge and the lasso, splines, trees, SVMs, PCA, and NCI60 clustering are in the lab.R scripts.
  • (*) means unsettled The README uses this mark when the author is not sure about that answer.
  • Links open this repository Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 use the local files. The upstream collection stays linked beside them.
